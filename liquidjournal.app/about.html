Why Liquid startedWhy Liquid started

17Years old when I started building Liquid

I started building Liquid at 17, not as a startup, as a lifeline.

On paper I was doing everything right, the YouTube videos, the courses, the mentors. Still unprofitable. For over a year I paid for prop firm evaluations every single month. I lost around $1,000 on them without one payout, and I was always in debt on them, down to a funded account I couldn’t even pay for.

I used other journals too. None of them gave me the data I wanted, the stats I wanted, or any satisfaction in using them.

Then it clicked: the problem was never my strategy. No strategy wins 100% of the time, and that’s exactly where 90% of traders break. They lose one trade, don’t know why, crash out for days… and never remember what made them take that F-tier setup in the first place.

Trading is one-on-one, you versus your own emotions. So I built the tool I wish I’d had: one that remembers every setup, every emotion, every mistake, with an AI coach that has read all of it, so the pattern quietly costing you money can’t hide anymore.

With Liquid I journaled more than I ever had, because this journal gave something back. Not magic, I finally stopped repeating the same mistakes, and I didn’t give up.

And I never wanted to make just another app. I wanted to find the traders going through what I went through, and build something great for them, so nobody has to figure this out alone.

If you’re buying another eval right now, hoping this one is different, I know that feeling.

And yes, there are paid tiers, and Apex isn’t cheap. Real AI that reads every trade you take costs real money to run, and I’d rather price it honestly than water it down. But the journal itself is free, forever, because the trader who needs this most is usually the one who can least afford another subscription. That was me.

So start free. Upgrade only once it’s already made you a better trader, never before.

Built by a trader who refused to quit, for the one who’s about to.
